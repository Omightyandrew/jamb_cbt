JAMB Final-Week Revision Plan

The last week is for consolidating what you have studied, checking the gaps that matter most and keeping your routine steady. This flexible seven-day framework is not a promise of a particular score or a replacement for earlier preparation.

Before you start the seven days

This is a final-review framework, not another long-range timetable. Adjust the amount of work to what you have already covered and how much time you can realistically study.

List your four subjects

Use your registered combination and current syllabus. Mark a few specific topics that need review in each subject.

Use retrieval before rereading

Close your notes and try to explain a concept, recall a definition or solve a problem. Then check your notes or an explanation and correct gaps.

Keep practice useful

Choose manageable question sets and review the reasoning behind missed or guessed answers. Do not treat repeated exposure as proof that an answer is memorized correctly.

A flexible seven-day revision framework

“Subject A–D” means your four registered subjects. Rotate the order to fit your needs; English should remain in the plan.

Day 1

Map and retrieve

Review your topic checklist. Try a short no-notes recall activity in each subject, then choose the most useful gaps to revisit this week.

Day 2

Revisit Subjects A and B

Review one weak area in each subject. Attempt focused questions and write down why any answer was wrong or uncertain.

Day 3

Revisit Subjects C and D

Use the same learn-retrieve-check cycle. Include English practice and any skill you tend to postpone.

Day 4

Return to difficult points

Revisit the errors from Days 2 and 3. Explain a method from memory, then use a fresh question to check understanding.

Day 5

Practise in mixed order

Complete a modest timed set across the four subjects if suitable. Follow the timing and navigation rules of the practice session, then review the result calmly.

Day 6

Consolidate, do not cram

Review concise notes, formulas or terms you have already studied. Correct a few remaining errors and stop adding large new topics.

Day 7

Light review and rest

Keep study light and familiar. Reduce workload, make time for normal meals and rest, and use the separate exam-day checklist to review practical arrangements against your current official notification. For changing requirements, check JAMB e-Facility.

Adapt the plan without overloading yourself

If you have less than seven days

Keep the same priorities but combine only short review tasks. Focus on the most useful topics you have already studied and protect rest.

If a subject feels much weaker

Give it an extra focused review, but do not remove every other subject from the rotation. Use questions to find a specific gap rather than reviewing everything indiscriminately.

If practice feels discouraging

Use mistakes as information. Separate a content gap from a reading or timing issue, review the method and try another suitable question when ready.

Use learning resources selectively

ExamPilot's syllabus and practice-question pages can help you find material to review. Choose resources that match your current study need rather than opening many new topics.

Frequently asked questions

Should I learn many new topics in the final week?

Prioritize consolidating what you have studied and addressing a few important gaps. Avoid trying to absorb a large amount of unfamiliar material at the last minute.

Should I practise all four JAMB subjects every day?

Not necessarily. Rotate your four registered subjects across the week and include English. Choose a pace that lets you retrieve, practise and review without overloading the day.

What should I do the day before JAMB?

Keep revision light, reduce workload and rest. Check your current official examination notification for practical requirements and use the exam-day checklist for general planning.
