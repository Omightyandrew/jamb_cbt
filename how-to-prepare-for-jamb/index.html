Understanding the Official UTME Examination Structure

Before designing a study schedule, candidates must understand exactly what the examination measures.

Standard 180-Question Format

According to the official Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board (JAMB) specifications, the examination comprises 180 multiple-choice questions: 60 questions in compulsory English Language and 40 questions in each of your three chosen elective subjects.

Strict 120-Minute Countdown

You have exactly two hours (120 minutes) to complete all four subjects. That gives you an average of approximately 40 seconds per question, meaning rapid reading comprehension and swift problem-solving are essential.

Authoritative 400-Mark Composite

Each of the four subjects carries an equal 25% weight toward your composite total out of 400. Neglecting any single subject can dramatically reduce your aggregate score and compromise your admission chances.

The 16-Week UTME Master Study Schedule

Divide your preparation into four progressive phases to avoid burnout and ensure deep conceptual retention.

Phase 1

Weeks 1–4: Foundation & Syllabus Mapping

Consult the official JAMB Syllabus for each of your four registered subjects. Read through the syllabus objectives and list every core topic. Gather your recommended textbooks and establish your daily study timetable.

Phase 2

Weeks 5–8: Intensive Topic Mastery

Cover two syllabus topics per subject each week. Read the source material, take concise summary notes, and immediately complete 20–30 topical questions on ExamPilot Practice Questions to reinforce the concepts.

Phase 3

Weeks 9–12: Speed Building & Past Questions

Transition from untimed topic study to timed subject drills. Work through historical JAMB Past Questions to identify recurring patterns, question phrasing styles, and examiner traps.

Phase 4

Weeks 13–16: Full CBT Simulation & Weak-Area Revision

Take full-length 180-question tests on CBT Mock Exams once or twice weekly. Spend the rest of the week reviewing your error log and revisiting troublesome topics with the AI Study Tutor.

Structuring Your Daily Study Sessions: The 2-Hour Block Method

Passive reading yields low retention. Structure your daily study hours using deliberate practice blocks.

Block 1: Deep Concept Learning (60 Minutes)

Focus entirely on one subject topic. Read the relevant chapter in an approved textbook, study worked examples, and explain key principles in your own words. Keep your phone out of reach.

Block 2: Active Question Practice (30 Minutes)

Close your textbook and attempt 25–30 topical questions on screen using JAMB CBT Practice. Avoid guessing; focus on verifying whether you genuinely understand the principles.

Block 3: Error Analysis & Correction (30 Minutes)

The most crucial step. Review every wrong or skipped answer. Write down the reason for the error in a dedicated revision notebook: was it a misread question, a forgotten formula, or a conceptual misunderstanding?
