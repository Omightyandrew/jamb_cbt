Broad areas to check against the syllabus

Atomic structure and periodic relationships

Review subatomic particles and how atomic structure relates to the organisation and properties of elements, to the level specified in the syllabus.

Chemical bonding and states of matter

Connect bonding and particle behaviour to the properties of substances and their physical states.

Chemical equations and the mole concept

Balance equations before using coefficients as mole ratios. Keep the distinction between particles, moles, and mass clear.

Acids, bases, and salts

Understand definitions, reactions, neutralisation, and salt formation. Apply quantitative methods only where the syllabus requires them.

Redox and electrolysis

Track electron transfer or oxidation-state changes. For electrolysis, identify the electrolyte, electrodes, concentration, and conditions before predicting products.

Organic chemistry

Practise recognising functional groups and relating structure to naming and properties where included in the official syllabus.

Environmental and practical-related areas

Study only the environmental, experimental, or applied content confirmed by the current syllabus. Interpret observations carefully and avoid unsupported extensions.

Separate conceptual knowledge from calculations

Conceptual questions

Explain what particles or electrons do, why a trend occurs, what a reaction observation indicates, and which conditions matter.

Calculation questions

Write a balanced equation, identify the relevant mole ratio, convert units, substitute values, and check that the answer's units match what is asked.

Keep a reaction and error record

Record conditions, balanced equations, and the reason a prior answer was incorrect. Revisit misconceptions rather than memorising only a final number.

Original worked examples

Each calculation below states its assumptions and uses a balanced equation where needed.

Balance a water-formation equation

2H2 + O2 → 2H2O. The equation has four hydrogen atoms and two oxygen atoms on each side. Its stoichiometric mole ratio H2:O2:H2O is 2:1:2.

Find a limiting reactant

Suppose 1.0 mol H2 reacts with 0.60 mol O2 under the equation above. The hydrogen requires 0.50 mol O2, so H2 is limiting; it forms 1.0 mol H2O and leaves 0.10 mol O2.

Calculate concentration

If 0.20 mol of solute is in 0.50 dm3 of solution, then c = n/V = 0.20/0.50 = 0.40 mol dm-3.

Neutralisation equation

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H2O is balanced. The equation gives a 1:1 mole ratio between HCl and NaOH.

Do not guess electrolysis products from the name alone

Products at the electrodes depend on the electrolyte, the electrode material, concentration, and operating conditions. Identify those details in the question before deciding which species are discharged. Do not apply a single product rule to every electrolyte.

Frequently asked questions

Which Chemistry areas should I revise?

Use the official current syllabus as your coverage checklist. The areas on this page are a preparation framework, not a verified ranking or complete syllabus.

How should I approach mole calculations?

Balance the equation, identify the mole ratio, convert the given quantity to moles where needed, and check units and limiting reactants.

Why should I balance an equation before using mole ratios?

The balanced coefficients represent the relative amounts of reacting and produced species. An unbalanced equation gives incorrect ratios.

What determines products during electrolysis?

Products depend on the electrolyte, electrode materials, concentration, and conditions. Consider all stated details before making a prediction.
