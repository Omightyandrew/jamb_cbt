Wellbeing during exam preparation

How to Manage JAMB Exam Anxiety and Stay Calm Under Pressure

Feeling nervous before an important examination can happen. You do not have to judge yourself for it. A steady preparation routine, practical day-of steps and support from people you trust can make the experience feel more manageable.

Build a preparation routine WHO stress-management guide

Practical support, not a diagnosis

This guide offers general study and coping suggestions. It does not diagnose a condition or replace help from a qualified health professional.

Reduce avoidable pressure while preparing

Small, repeatable steps can make revision more predictable without promising a particular result.

Make the next task manageable

Choose one syllabus topic or practice set for the session. A clear next step is easier to start than trying to revise every subject at once.

Practise the process gradually

Begin with untimed questions while learning. Add short timed practice when ready, then review your answers. The goal is familiarity with the process, not proving your worth through a score.

Prepare practical details early

Check your current official examination notification and make a travel plan in advance. Avoid relying on last-minute rumours about centre rules or requirements.

Protect rest and routine

Keep a regular sleep opportunity and take breaks that help you return to study. Avoid an all-night cram session as a substitute for preparation.

A simple grounding routine to try

Use these gentle steps if you notice tension rising. Stop if a technique feels uncomfortable; there is no need to force a particular breathing pattern.

1

Pause and settle

If it feels comfortable, place both feet on the floor and let your shoulders rest. Notice the support beneath you.

2

Notice the present

Silently name a few things you can see, hear and feel. Bring attention back to the room rather than trying to predict every possible outcome.

3

Breathe gently

Take a few comfortable, unforced breaths. If focusing on breathing is not helpful, return attention to your surroundings or speak with someone you trust.

4

Choose one next action

Return to one question, one instruction or one practical task. You can handle the next step without solving the entire examination at once.

If a question feels difficult during the exam

Read the instruction again

Take a moment to identify what the question asks. If it remains unclear or difficult, follow the examination interface and invigilator instructions for moving on and returning where allowed.

Focus on the item in front of you

Do not let one difficult question define the whole paper. Work through the next available step and use the time according to the official examination instructions.

Ask for help with procedure, not answers

If there is a technical or procedural problem, alert an invigilator. Follow their directions and do not seek or accept help with examination answers.

When to reach out for support

If distress feels intense, keeps returning, disrupts sleep or daily life, or feels difficult to manage alone, tell a trusted adult, teacher, school counsellor or qualified health professional. Seeking support is a sensible step, not a failure.

Frequently asked questions

Is it wrong to feel nervous before JAMB?

No. Nervousness can happen before an important event. It does not by itself say anything about your ability or determine your result.

What can I do if I panic over a difficult question?

Pause if the instructions allow, read the item carefully, use a comfortable grounding step and move on or return as permitted by the exam interface and invigilator directions.

When should I talk to someone about exam stress?

If distress is persistent, severe or interfering with everyday life, speak with a trusted person or qualified professional for support.
