JAMB Study Timetable for Science, Commercial and Arts Students

A useful timetable is built around the subjects you actually registered for, the time you have, and the topics that need attention. Use these weekly examples as flexible starting points, not fixed rules for every student in a stream.

Review the JAMB syllabus Check subject combinations

Start with your four subjects

Use your current JAMB registration and official subject-combination guidance. Stream labels are only broad descriptions; they do not determine every candidate's subjects.

Set up a timetable you can follow

Plan repeatable sessions rather than filling every free hour. Keep all four registered subjects in the rotation and include question practice and answer review.

Confirm your subjects

Use the current official JAMB brochure or IBASS guidance for your intended course. English is one of the four UTME subjects; verify the other three rather than assuming a standard Science, Commercial or Arts combination.

Choose realistic blocks

Begin with one or two focused sessions that fit around school, work, family responsibilities and rest. A 30–45 minute block is a practical example, not a required duration.

Give each session a task

For example, review one syllabus area, solve a small set of relevant questions, then write down what you need to revisit. Do not count rereading alone as proof of mastery.

Three adaptable weekly examples

In these examples, English is compulsory and Electives A, B and C stand for the candidate's own registered subjects. Adjust the study emphasis to the work each subject requires.

Science-focused week

  • Monday: English reading practice and Elective A concepts.
  • Tuesday: Elective B worked problems; check each step.
  • Wednesday: English vocabulary or grammar and Elective C.
  • Thursday: Elective A questions and Elective B error review.
  • Friday: Elective C questions and a short mixed recall drill.
  • Saturday: Timed practice across selected subjects, then review errors.
  • Sunday: Rest, or a brief catch-up if needed.

For calculation-heavy areas, show working, check units and revisit the concept behind an incorrect answer.

Commercial-focused week

  • Monday: English comprehension and Elective A concepts.
  • Tuesday: Elective B calculations, records or data interpretation as applicable.
  • Wednesday: English lexis or structure and Elective C recall.
  • Thursday: Elective A practice and review of Elective B errors.
  • Friday: Elective C questions and a short mixed recall drill.
  • Saturday: Timed subject practice, followed by explanation and correction review.
  • Sunday: Rest, or a brief catch-up if needed.

Use the syllabus and your registered subjects to decide which concepts, calculations or interpretations belong in each block.

Arts-focused week

  • Monday: English comprehension and Elective A key concepts.
  • Tuesday: Elective B terms, themes or chronology as applicable.
  • Wednesday: English oral or language practice and Elective C.
  • Thursday: Elective A questions and Elective B recall without notes.
  • Friday: Elective C questions and a short mixed recall drill.
  • Saturday: Timed practice across selected subjects, then review errors.
  • Sunday: Rest, or a brief catch-up if needed.

Practise precise interpretation of questions and retrieve facts or concepts from memory before checking notes.

These are rotation examples, not official subject combinations or prescribed JAMB schedules. Replace the elective labels with your own registered subjects and verify current requirements through the official JAMB IBASS portal.

Adapt it to your week

1

When school takes most of the day

Use one short session on busy weekdays and a longer practice-and-review block when you are less rushed. Keep sleep and essential responsibilities in the plan.

2

When study time is limited

Rotate subjects over the week instead of trying to cover all four every day. Make each session specific: one topic, a small practice set, and a correction.

3

When one subject is weaker

Add another focused contact with that subject next week, but keep the other three in rotation. Review the reason for errors before simply increasing question volume.

4

When you miss a session

Move its priority task to an available slot or shorten a later session. Avoid doubling every block the next day and creating an unsustainable plan.

Frequently asked questions

Do all Science, Commercial and Arts students take the same JAMB subjects?

No. Subject requirements depend on the intended course and current JAMB guidance. Confirm your own combination rather than relying on a stream label.

How many hours should I study each day?

There is no single daily target that fits everyone. Choose manageable sessions around your responsibilities, then check whether you are learning, practising and reviewing consistently.

What should I do if I fall behind my timetable?

Prioritize the next important topic and reschedule one task at a time. Avoid trying to make up every missed hour in one day.
