How Nigerian Universities Calculate Post-UTME Aggregate Scores

Your JAMB UTME score is rarely evaluated in isolation. Nigerian universities combine UTME scores, institutional Post-UTME screening tests, and O'Level grades into a composite aggregate score—using formulas set independently by each university's Senate.

Key Fact

There is no single universal aggregate formula across Nigerian universities. Every institution's Senate determines its own weighting criteria and updates them per admission cycle.

Why There Is No Universal National Aggregate Formula

Under Nigerian tertiary education regulations, institutional Senates retain full autonomy over their internal screening criteria.

Different Screening Formats

Some universities conduct computer-based or written Post-UTME aptitude examinations, while others conduct non-examination screenings using only JAMB UTME scores and verified O'Level grades.

Varying Component Weights

Institutions assign different percentage weights to your UTME score, your Post-UTME test result, and your five core O'Level subjects. An aggregate calculated under one university's method cannot be applied to another institution.

Cycle-by-Cycle Senate Reviews

A university's Senate may adjust component weights or screening pass marks between academic sessions. Always consult your chosen institution's current official Post-UTME screening notice before estimating your aggregate.

Documented Institutional Example: University of Lagos (2025/2026 Session)

In its published 2025/2026 Post-UTME admission guidelines, the University of Lagos (UNILAG) structured its screening evaluation around three core weighted components totaling 100%:

1. JAMB UTME Score (50%)

The candidate's UTME score out of 400 contributes half of the composite admission assessment. Mathematically, scaling a 400-point score down to a 50-point scale corresponds to dividing the UTME score by 8 (e.g., 280 ÷ 8 = 35.0 out of 50).

2. Post-UTME Screening Test (30%)

The university's online Post-UTME screening examination accounts for 30% of the final aggregate score.

3. O'Level Grades (20%)

Verified O'Level results (WAEC, NECO, or equivalent) in the five core required subjects account for the remaining 20%. Always verify the exact grade-point conversion rules published directly in UNILAG's official admission notice for your specific intake session.

Checking Formulas for Other Universities (Including UI and State Institutions)

Never rely on unofficial blogs or unverified third-party calculators to determine your screening score.

Verify Current UI Screening Notices

Institutions such as the University of Ibadan (UI) publish their exact screening structure, eligibility cut-offs, and scoring rules directly in their official admission notices for each academic session. Check UI's official admissions portal directly rather than assuming historical formulas still apply.

Check IBASS & Institutional Bulletins

Before sitting for any screening, verify both your O'Level sitting policies (single sitting vs two sittings) and subject prerequisites on the JAMB IBASS System and your target university's official registrar notices.
