Understanding JAMB Cut-Off Marks: National Minimum Benchmarks vs Institutional Cut-Offs

Every admission cycle, candidates confuse the national minimum policy benchmark approved at the JAMB Policy Meeting with the actual merit scores required to gain admission into specific universities and competitive degree programmes.

Key Principle

Meeting JAMB's national minimum benchmark only establishes basic eligibility to be considered for admission screening. It does not guarantee admission into any university, polytechnic, or specific course.

The 2026/2027 JAMB National Minimum Admission Benchmarks

At the annual Policy Meeting on Admissions for the 2026/2027 academic session (held on May 11, 2026), JAMB and heads of Nigerian tertiary institutions approved the following minimum tolerable UTME benchmarks:

Universities: 150

No Nigerian university (federal, state, or private) is permitted to admit any candidate scoring below 150 in the 2026/2027 UTME. Note that most competitive public universities independently require higher minimum scores (commonly 180 or 200) to sit for their Post-UTME screening.

Colleges of Nursing Sciences: 150

For the 2026/2027 admission session, the national minimum benchmark for Colleges of Nursing Sciences was standardized at 150 (up from the 140 benchmark recorded in the historical 2025/2026 session of July 8, 2025).

Polytechnics & Colleges of Education: 100

The national minimum floor for Polytechnics, Monotechnics, Colleges of Education, and Colleges of Agriculture remains 100 for the 2026/2027 cycle (consistent with the historical 2025/2026 policy floor).

Three Levels of Cut-Off Marks in Nigerian Admissions

To assess your admission chances accurately, you must distinguish between national policy floors, institutional minimums, and departmental merit cut-offs.

1. National Minimum Policy Benchmark

Set collectively by the Federal Ministry of Education, JAMB, Vice-Chancellors, Rectors, and Provosts at the annual Policy Meeting. It serves purely as a regulatory floor below which institutions cannot admit.

2. Institutional General Cut-Off Mark

Each tertiary institution’s Senate or Academic Board exercises autonomy to raise its institutional entry threshold above the national minimum. For example, while the national benchmark is 150, a university Senate may require at least 200 to apply for its Post-UTME screening.

3. Departmental & Course-Specific Cut-Offs

Determined after Post-UTME screening based on applicant volume, institutional carrying capacity, and aggregate scores. High-demand courses (such as Medicine, Law, Pharmacy, and Computer Science) carry significantly higher departmental cut-offs than less competitive disciplines.

Why Previous Years' Cut-Offs Are Never Guarantees

Departmental cut-off marks fluctuate every admission session based on competitive demand.

Applicant Pool Competition

If an exceptionally large number of high-scoring candidates apply for a single department in a given year, the departmental merit cut-off automatically rises to fit the accredited carrying capacity.

No Automatic Admission Guarantee

Meeting either JAMB's national minimum (150) or an institution's general Post-UTME eligibility mark only permits you to participate in screening. Admission depends on your final composite ranking, O'Level prerequisites, and quota availability.
