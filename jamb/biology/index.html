Subject Overview

JAMB Biology is a compulsory requirement for medicine, nursing, pharmacy, dentistry, medical laboratory science, microbiology, and agricultural sciences. The 40 questions emphasize cell organization, enzyme kinetics, circulatory and digestive systems, Mendelian genetics, ecological biomes, and adaptation strategies.

Key Topics for JAMB Biology

These core topic clusters feature regularly across UTME past questions and curriculum guidelines.

Cell Structure & Membrane Transport

Organelles (mitochondria, chloroplasts, ribosomes), diffusion, osmosis, and active transport.

Genetics & Inheritance

Mendelian monohybrid/dihybrid crosses, sex-linked traits, ABO blood groups, and gene mutations.

Ecology & Ecosystem Dynamics

Trophic levels, energy flow, food webs, ecological succession, and conservation of natural resources.

Human Physiology & Regulation

Circulation, respiration, excretion (kidney nephron), nervous coordination, and hormonal balance.

Plant Physiology & Photosynthesis

Light and dark reactions, transpiration in xylem, phloem translocation, and tropisms.

Sample JAMB Biology Questions & Explanations

Review these verified practice questions with step-by-step reasoning to understand how questions are framed in the UTME.

Question 1 · Cell Biology (Organelles)

Which cell organelle is primarily responsible for the synthesis of adenosine triphosphate (ATP) during aerobic cellular respiration?

A. Ribosome
B. Endoplasmic reticulum
C. Mitochondrion
D. Golgi body
Correct Answer: C — Mitochondria are the "powerhouses" of eukaryotic cells, generating the bulk of chemical energy in the form of ATP via the Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation.
Question 2 · Plant Physiology (Vascular Tissues)

The plant tissue responsible for the translocation of synthesized organic food substances from leaves to other storage organs is the:

A. Xylem
B. Phloem
C. Cambium
D. Sclerenchyma
Correct Answer: B — Phloem transports dissolved photosynthates (sucrose and amino acids) bidirectionally, whereas xylem carries water and mineral salts from roots upward.
Question 3 · Genetics (Mendelian Inheritance)

In a monohybrid cross between two heterozygous tall pea plants (Tt × Tt), what is the expected phenotypic ratio of the offspring?

A. 1 tall : 1 dwarf
B. 2 tall : 1 dwarf
C. 3 tall : 1 dwarf
D. 4 tall : 0 dwarf
Correct Answer: C — Crossing Tt × Tt yields genotypes TT (1), Tt (2), tt (1). Because T is dominant over t, 3 out of 4 plants will display the tall phenotype, giving a 3:1 phenotypic ratio.
Question 4 · Ecology (Symbiosis)

An association between two different organisms in which one benefits while the other is neither harmed nor helped is termed:

A. Mutualism
B. Commensalism
C. Parasitism
D. Predation
Correct Answer: B — Commensalism is a symbiotic relationship where one species benefits without causing harm or benefit to the host (for example, barnacles on whales or remoras on sharks).
Question 5 · Human Physiology (Excretion)

The structural and functional unit of the human kidney responsible for filtering blood and forming urine is the:

A. Neuron
B. Alveolus
C. Nephron
D. Glomerulus only
Correct Answer: C — The nephron is the complete functional microscopic unit of the kidney, comprising the Bowman's capsule, glomerulus, loop of Henle, and collecting duct.
