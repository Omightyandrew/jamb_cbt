Subject Overview

JAMB History is tailored for candidates intending to study History and International Studies, Law, Archaeology, Political Science, Diplomacy, and Education. The 40 questions assess knowledge of ancient African kingdoms, European exploration and colonization, Lugard's Indirect Rule system, constitutional conferences, and Nigeria's political evolution.

Key Topics for JAMB History

These core topic clusters feature regularly across UTME past questions and curriculum guidelines.

Pre-Colonial Nigerian States

Sokoto Caliphate and 1804 Jihad, Kanem-Borno Empire, Benin Kingdom art and structure, and Old Oyo Empire.

European Exploration & Trade Contacts

Exploration of River Niger (Mungo Park, Lander Brothers), the Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade, and legitimate commerce.

Colonial Conquest & Amalgamation (1914)

British treaties, Berlin Conference (1884-1885), Frederick Lugard, and the amalgamation of Northern and Southern protectorates.

Nationalism & Political Parties

Herbert Macaulay and NNDP, Nnamdi Azikiwe and NCNC, Action Group (AG), NPC, and independence in 1960.

Post-Independence Crises & Civil War

The First Republic, 1966 military coups, the Nigerian Civil War (1967-1970), and Second Republic politics.

Sample JAMB History Questions & Explanations

Review these verified practice questions with step-by-step reasoning to understand how questions are framed in the UTME.

Question 1 · Pre-Colonial History (Sokoto Caliphate)

The great 1804 Islamic reform movement in Hausaland that culminated in the establishment of the Sokoto Caliphate was led by:

A. Muhammad Bello
B. Sheikh Usman dan Fodio
C. Abdullahi dan Fodio
D. Mai Idris Alooma
Correct Answer: B — Usman dan Fodio led the 1804 Jihad in Gobir, uniting the Hausa states into the Sokoto Caliphate and writing numerous treatises on Islamic governance.
Question 2 · Colonial Administration (Amalgamation)

In what year did the British colonial administration amalgamate the Northern and Southern Protectorates into unified Nigeria under Lord Frederick Lugard?

A. 1900
B. 1906
C. 1914
D. 1922
Correct Answer: C — On January 1, 1914, Lord Frederick Lugard amalgamated the Colony and Protectorate of Southern Nigeria with the Protectorate of Northern Nigeria.
Question 3 · Nationalism (First Political Party)

Which organization is historically recognized as the first political party established in Nigeria in 1923 by Herbert Macaulay?

A. Nigerian Youth Movement (NYM)
B. Nigerian National Democratic Party (NNDP)
C. National Council of Nigeria and the Cameroons (NCNC)
D. Action Group (AG)
Correct Answer: B — The NNDP was founded in 1923 by Herbert Macaulay to contest the newly introduced legislative seats in Lagos under the Clifford Constitution.
Question 4 · Colonial Resistance

The widespread 1929 protest by market women in Eastern Nigeria against colonial tax policies and warrant chiefs is known as the:

A. Abeokuta Women's Revolt
B. Aba Women's War (Ogu Umunwanyi)
C. Enugu Colliery Strike
D. Ekumeku Movement
Correct Answer: B — The Aba Women's War of November-December 1929 was an organized revolt against direct taxation and the oppressive authority of colonial warrant chiefs.
Question 5 · Post-Independence (Republican Status)

Nigeria ceased to be a British Dominion and became an independent Federal Republic with its own President in:

A. 1960
B. 1963
C. 1966
D. 1979
Correct Answer: B — On October 1, 1963, Nigeria enacted its Republican Constitution, replacing the British Monarch with Dr. Nnamdi Azikiwe as ceremonial President.
