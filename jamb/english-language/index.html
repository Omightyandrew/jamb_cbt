Subject Overview

In the Unified Tertiary Matriculation Examination (UTME), Use of English is taken by all candidates regardless of faculty. The paper consists of 60 questions covering Comprehension and Summary passages, Lexis and Structure (sentence completion, synonyms, antonyms, idioms), Oral English forms (vowels, consonants, word stress), and the prescribed literary text. Scoring high in English provides a critical boost to your overall aggregate score.

Key Topics for JAMB English Language

These core topic clusters feature regularly across UTME past questions and curriculum guidelines.

Concord & Subject-Verb Agreement

Rules governing singular/plural subjects, correlative conjunctions (either...or, neither...nor), and collective nouns.

Synonyms, Antonyms & Register

Identifying words nearest or opposite in meaning within specific contextual passages.

Oral English & Word Stress

Syllabic stress patterns in multisyllabic words, emphatic stress, and vowel/consonant sound distinctions.

Reading Comprehension & Inference

Deducing implied meanings, tone, author attitude, and extracting central themes under timed conditions.

Prescribed Literature Reading Text

Thorough knowledge of characters, plots, themes, and key incidents from the official JAMB reading novel.

Sample JAMB English Language Questions & Explanations

Review these verified practice questions with step-by-step reasoning to understand how questions are framed in the UTME.

Question 1 · Concord (Proximity Rule)

Choose the option that correctly completes the sentence: "Neither the teacher nor the students _______ present at the orientation seminar."

A. was
B. were
C. is
D. are being
Correct Answer: B — According to the rule of proximity for correlative conjunctions ("neither... nor"), the verb agrees in number with the subject closest to it. "Students" is plural, so the plural past verb "were" is correct.
Question 2 · Lexis (Synonyms in Context)

Choose the option nearest in meaning to the capitalized word: "His EPHEMERAL fame faded as quickly as the trend died down."

A. long-lasting
B. short-lived
C. suspicious
D. immense
Correct Answer: B — "Ephemeral" means lasting for a very short time or transitory. Therefore, "short-lived" is the closest synonym.
Question 3 · Oral English (Word Stress)

Which syllable carries the primary stress in the word: PHOTOGRAPHIC?

A. PHO-to-graph-ic
B. pho-TO-graph-ic
C. pho-to-GRAPH-ic
D. pho-to-graph-IC
Correct Answer: C — Words ending in the suffix "-ic" normally carry primary stress on the penultimate (second to last) syllable: pho-to-GRAPH-ic (stress on the third syllable).
Question 4 · Idiomatic Expressions

Select the option that best explains the underlined idiom: "Amina has been <u>burning the candle at both ends</u> since the beginning of the semester."

A. Wasting candle wax during night prep
B. Working very hard with little rest from early morning to late night
C. Neglecting her academic assignments
D. Trying to save money on electricity bills
Correct Answer: B — "To burn the candle at both ends" means to exhaust one's energy by working excessively hard or staying up very late and waking up early.
Question 5 · Prepositional Complementation

Choose the correct preposition: "The jury acquitted the defendant _______ all criminal charges."

A. from
B. with
C. of
D. against
Correct Answer: C — The standard idiomatic collocation in English grammar is to "acquit someone of a crime or charge."
