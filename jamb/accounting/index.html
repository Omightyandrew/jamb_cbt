Subject Overview

JAMB Accounting (Financial Accounting) is a core requirement for candidates applying for Accounting, Finance, Taxation, Banking and Finance, and Actuarial Science. The 40 questions test fundamental accounting concepts, trial balance preparation, correction of errors, year-end adjustments (depreciation, accruals, prepayments), and balance sheet analysis.

Key Topics for JAMB Accounting

These core topic clusters feature regularly across UTME past questions and curriculum guidelines.

Accounting Concepts & Conventions

Going concern, accruals/matching, prudence (conservatism), consistency, materiality, and entity concepts.

Books of Original Entry & Ledger

Sales, purchases, returns journals, petty cash book, and the three-column cash book.

Trial Balance & Correction of Errors

Errors affecting trial balance agreement versus errors not affecting it (commission, omission, principle).

Bank Reconciliation Statements

Adjusted cash book, unpresented cheques, uncredited lodgements, bank charges, and standing orders.

Final Accounts & Adjustments

Trading account, Profit and Loss statement, Balance sheet, depreciation methods, and provision for doubtful debts.

Sample JAMB Accounting Questions & Explanations

Review these verified practice questions with step-by-step reasoning to understand how questions are framed in the UTME.

Question 1 · Accounting Concepts (The Accrual Concept)

Which fundamental accounting concept requires revenue and expenses to be recognized and recorded in the period they occur, rather than when cash is transferred?

A. Money measurement concept
B. Prudence concept
C. Accrual (matching) concept
D. Going concern concept
Correct Answer: C — The accrual (or matching) concept states that income earned and expenses incurred in a given financial period must be recorded in that period, regardless of cash receipt or payment.
Question 2 · Correction of Errors

If the purchase of a motor van for ₦2,000,000 was mistakenly debited to the Motor Expenses Account, what type of error has occurred?

A. Error of commission
B. Error of principle
C. Error of omission
D. Compensating error
Correct Answer: B — An error of principle occurs when an entry violates fundamental accounting principles, such as recording capital expenditure (an asset) as revenue expenditure (an expense).
Question 3 · Bank Reconciliation Statements

In a bank reconciliation statement prepared starting with the balance as per adjusted Cash Book, an unpresented cheque should be:

A. Added
B. Deducted
C. Ignored
D. Multiplied by interest
Correct Answer: A — Unpresented cheques are cheques drawn and credited in the cash book that have not yet cleared the bank. To reconcile starting from the cash book balance to the bank statement balance, unpresented cheques are added.
Question 4 · Final Accounts (Cost of Goods Sold)

Given: Opening Inventory = ₦40,000, Purchases = ₦250,000, Carriage Inwards = ₦10,000, Closing Inventory = ₦60,000. Calculate Cost of Goods Sold (COGS).

A. ₦240,000
B. ₦250,000
C. ₦260,000
D. ₦300,000
Correct Answer: A — COGS = Opening Stock (₦40,000) + Purchases (₦250,000) + Carriage Inwards (₦10,000) - Closing Stock (₦60,000) = ₦300,000 - ₦60,000 = ₦240,000.
Question 5 · Accounting Ratios (Acid Test)

A company has Current Assets of ₦800,000, Inventory of ₦300,000, and Current Liabilities of ₦250,000. What is its Quick (Acid-Test) Ratio?

A. 1.2 : 1
B. 2.0 : 1
C. 2.5 : 1
D. 3.2 : 1
Correct Answer: B — Quick Ratio = (Current Assets - Closing Inventory) / Current Liabilities = (₦800,000 - ₦300,000) / ₦250,000 = ₦500,000 / ₦250,000 = 2.0 : 1.
