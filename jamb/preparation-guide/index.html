JAMB Preparation Guide

A structured preparation plan connects learning with practice. Begin by identifying the subjects you need to prepare for, then use a realistic routine to learn concepts, practise regularly, review incorrect answers and improve the areas that need more attention.

Know what to prepare

Confirm the subjects relevant to your plan and use a syllabus outline to organise the topics you need to study.

Build a realistic routine

Set manageable study sessions that leave time for learning, question practice, review and returning to difficult areas.

Learn before guessing

Understand the concepts behind a question before relying on repeated attempts or memorised answers.

Review and improve

Use incorrect answers as revision prompts and adjust your next study session around the gaps you find.

Start with the syllabus

Understanding the relevant syllabus and topic areas gives your preparation a direction before intensive question practice. Use it to divide broad subjects into focused study sessions without reproducing or replacing official syllabus guidance.

Practice JAMB Questions

Regular question practice helps you apply what you have learned, notice knowledge gaps, become familiar with question formats and develop useful time-management habits. It should support learning rather than promise a particular score.

Practise by subject

Choose a subject or topic that matches the part of the syllabus you are studying.

Explore Practice Questions

Attempt carefully

Read each question, consider the options and practise working within the time available to you.

Find knowledge gaps

Use questions you miss or cannot explain as signals for another learning and review session.

Review past questions

Past questions can add context to preparation by giving you previous-question material to work through and review. Use them alongside syllabus study and do not assume they predict the exact questions that will appear in a future examination.

Brochure and admission planning

Relevant course and admission information can help you plan which subjects and topics deserve attention. Use brochure resources as a starting point, link them with subject-combination guidance, and verify changing requirements with appropriate current official sources.

Plan → Learn → Practice → Review → Improve → Prepare

Use this simple cycle to keep preparation purposeful and make each stage lead into the next.

1

Plan

Choose subjects, topics and study sessions that fit your available time.

2

Learn

Study the concepts and examples related to the topic you selected.

3

Practice

Attempt questions and practise applying what you have learned.

4

Review

Check answers and explanations where available, then identify errors.

5

Improve

Return to weak areas and change your next session where necessary.

6

Prepare

Repeat the cycle and build comfort with CBT-style question practice.

CBT preparation

Prepare for computer-based practice by answering questions on screen, becoming comfortable navigating between questions, managing available time, reading carefully and reviewing answers where appropriate. Consistent practice can help you become more familiar with the process.
