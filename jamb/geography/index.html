Subject Overview

JAMB Geography is required for students entering Geography, Surveying and Geoinformatics, Urban and Regional Planning, Environmental Management, and Estate Management. The 40 questions test topographical map reading, earth structure, rock types, atmospheric pressure systems, vegetation zones, and population dynamics.

Key Topics for JAMB Geography

These core topic clusters feature regularly across UTME past questions and curriculum guidelines.

Map Work & Cartography

Scale conversion, contour interpretation, grid references, relief representation, intervisibility, and gradient calculation.

Earth Structure & Geomorphology

Crust, mantle, core, plate tectonics, rock classification (igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic), and weathering.

Weather & World Climates

Atmospheric pressure, Koppen climate classification, planetary winds, rainfall types, and global warming.

Soils & Vegetation Zones of Nigeria

Mangrove swamp, rainforest, guinea savanna, sudan savanna, and sahel savanna distribution.

Human Geography & Settlement

Rural and urban settlement patterns, migration factors, transport networks, and mineral resource distribution.

Sample JAMB Geography Questions & Explanations

Review these verified practice questions with step-by-step reasoning to understand how questions are framed in the UTME.

Question 1 · Cartography (Map Scale)

If a topographical map has a representative fraction of 1:50,000, what actual ground distance in kilometers is represented by 4 cm on the map?

A. 1 km
B. 2 km
C. 4 km
D. 20 km
Correct Answer: B — At 1:50,000 scale, 1 cm on the map = 50,000 cm = 500 m = 0.5 km. Therefore, 4 cm on the map = 4 × 0.5 km = 2.0 km on the ground.
Question 2 · Physical Geography (Rocks)

Rocks that form directly from the cooling and crystallization of molten magma beneath the earth's surface are classified as:

A. Sedimentary rocks
B. Plutonic (intrusive) igneous rocks
C. Metamorphic rocks
D. Volcanic (extrusive) igneous rocks
Correct Answer: B — Intrusive or plutonic igneous rocks (such as granite and gabbro) solidify slowly deep within the Earth's crust, forming large coarse crystals.
Question 3 · Climatology (Instruments)

Which meteorological instrument is used by scientists to measure atmospheric pressure?

A. Anemometer
B. Hygrometer
C. Barometer
D. Wind vane
Correct Answer: C — Atmospheric pressure is measured using a mercury or aneroid barometer. An anemometer measures wind speed, and a hygrometer measures humidity.
Question 4 · River Landforms

An ox-bow lake is a crescent-shaped water body typically formed during which stage of a river's profile?

A. Upper or youth stage
B. Middle or mature stage
C. Lower or old stage
D. Torrential stage
Correct Answer: C — Ox-bow lakes form in the lower/old stage of a river when continuous erosion and deposition at the neck of a pronounced meander cut off the bend.
Question 5 · Regional Geography of Nigeria

The geographic confluence of the River Niger and River Benue is located in which historic Nigerian city?

A. Makurdi, Benue State
B. Lokoja, Kogi State
C. Jebba, Kwara State
D. Onitsha, Anambra State
Correct Answer: B — Lokoja, the capital of Kogi State, is situated at the historic confluence where the Rivers Niger and Benue merge.
