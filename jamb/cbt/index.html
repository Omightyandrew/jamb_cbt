Practice before the exam

Regular practice can help you become more familiar with computer-based question sessions, notice knowledge gaps and develop habits for reading carefully and managing available time. It does not guarantee a score or result in the official examination.

Learn the content first

Use learning and syllabus review to understand concepts before relying on repeated question attempts.

Practise consistently

Choose manageable sessions that fit your routine and return to topics that need more work.

Use review as feedback

Check answers where available and turn mistakes into prompts for your next study session.

The JAMB syllabus can help you organise subjects and topic areas before you begin intensive practice. Use it to decide what to learn, which question set fits your current study and what to revisit after review.

Learn → Practice → Review → Improve → Repeat

Use a simple cycle to connect understanding with question practice and make your next study session purposeful.

1

Learn

Study the concepts and topic areas relevant to your current plan.

2

Practice

Answer questions on screen and work through the session carefully.

3

Review

Check your answers and explanations where they are available.

4

Improve

Return to weak topics and adjust what you study next.

5

Repeat

Continue the cycle consistently as you build preparation habits.

CBT preparation habits

Small habits can make computer-based practice more deliberate without making promises about official results.

Read on screen carefully

Take time to understand the question and all available options before selecting an answer.

Navigate deliberately

Become comfortable moving through questions and returning to items that need another review.

Manage your available time

Practise pacing yourself during sessions without assuming that practice settings match official examination conditions.

Keep practising

Use a consistent routine and connect each session to syllabus study and answer review.
