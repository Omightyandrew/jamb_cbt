Subject Overview

JAMB Economics is required for students aiming for Economics, Accounting, Business Administration, Banking and Finance, Marketing, and Social Sciences. The 40 questions balance theoretical concepts (scarcity, opportunity cost, market structures) with quantitative problems (elasticity, national income components, fiscal policy, inflation, and balance of payments).

Key Topics for JAMB Economics

These core topic clusters feature regularly across UTME past questions and curriculum guidelines.

Scarcity, Choice & Opportunity Cost

Production possibility curves (PPC), economic systems, scale of preference, and resource allocation.

Demand, Supply & Price Determination

Laws of demand and supply, shifts versus movements, equilibrium price, and elasticity of demand and supply.

Market Structures & Firm Theory

Perfect competition, monopoly, oligopoly, monopolistic competition, and profit-maximization rules.

National Income Accounting

GDP, GNP, NNP calculations, circular flow of income, per capita income, and measurement problems in developing nations.

Money, Banking & Central Bank Monetary Policy

Commercial bank credit creation, Central Bank instruments (Cash Reserve Ratio, Open Market Operations, MPR), and inflation types.

Sample JAMB Economics Questions & Explanations

Review these verified practice questions with step-by-step reasoning to understand how questions are framed in the UTME.

Question 1 · Basic Concepts (Opportunity Cost)

In economics, the real cost of buying a new textbook instead of attending a cinema show is measured by the:

A. Monetary price of the textbook
B. Utility and satisfaction derived from the cinema show forgone
C. Cost of production of the textbook
D. Total cash saved in the student's wallet
Correct Answer: B — Opportunity cost is the next best alternative forgone when a choice is made. Here, it is the satisfaction of the cinema show that was sacrificed to buy the book.
Question 2 · Elasticity of Demand

When the price of a commodity increases by 10%, the quantity demanded falls by 25%. What is the price elasticity of demand?

A. 0.4
B. 1.0
C. 2.5
D. 3.5
Correct Answer: C — Price Elasticity of Demand = |% change in quantity demanded / % change in price| = |-25% / 10%| = 2.5 (elastic demand).
Question 3 · National Income Accounting

The key statistical difference between Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and Gross National Product (GNP) is:

A. Depreciation allowances
B. Net factor income from abroad
C. Indirect business taxes
D. Total government subsidies
Correct Answer: B — GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA). GDP measures output within domestic borders, whereas GNP measures output by citizens regardless of location.
Question 4 · Monetary Policy Instruments

The Central Bank tool involving the deliberate buying and selling of government treasury bills to regulate liquidity in the economy is known as:

A. Moral suasion
B. Open Market Operations (OMO)
C. Minimum Reserve Requirement
D. Selective credit control
Correct Answer: B — Open Market Operations (OMO) refers to the purchase or sale of government securities by the Central Bank to influence commercial bank liquidity and credit volume.
Question 5 · Market Structures

Under which market structure does a firm face a completely horizontal (perfectly elastic) demand curve?

A. Monopoly
B. Oligopoly
C. Perfect competition
D. Monopolistic competition
Correct Answer: C — In a perfectly competitive market, individual firms are price takers with negligible market share, facing a horizontal demand curve where Price = Marginal Revenue.
