Subject Overview

JAMB Government is an essential requirement for Law, Political Science, Public Administration, Mass Communication, International Relations, and Sociology. The 40 exam questions test concepts of power and authority, pre-colonial administration, Nigerian constitutional milestones from 1922 to 1999, electoral systems, and Africa's role in global diplomacy.

Key Topics for JAMB Government

These core topic clusters feature regularly across UTME past questions and curriculum guidelines.

Basic Political Concepts

Sovereignty, Rule of Law, Separation of Powers, Legitimacy, and Forms of Government (Federal, Unitary, Confederal).

Pre-Colonial Administrative Systems

Emirate system in the North, Oyo Empire's checks and balances, and the Igbo acephalous/republican structure.

Colonial & Pre-Independence Constitutions

Clifford (1922), Richards (1946), Macpherson (1951), and Lyttelton (1954) federal foundations.

Post-Independence Constitutions

Independence (1960), Republican (1963), Second Republic (1979), and Fourth Republic (1999) constitutions.

International Organizations & Nigerian Foreign Policy

ECOWAS, African Union (AU), United Nations (UN), and the Commonwealth of Nations.

Sample JAMB Government Questions & Explanations

Review these verified practice questions with step-by-step reasoning to understand how questions are framed in the UTME.

Question 1 · Constitutional History (Elective Principle)

Which colonial constitution introduced the elective principle into Nigerian politics for the first time?

A. Richards Constitution of 1946
B. Clifford Constitution of 1922
C. Macpherson Constitution of 1951
D. Lyttelton Constitution of 1954
Correct Answer: B — The Clifford Constitution of 1922 introduced the elective principle, creating four elected legislative seats (three for Lagos and one for Calabar).
Question 2 · Political Theory (Separation of Powers)

The doctrine of Separation of Powers was famously formulated by which political philosopher to guard against tyranny?

A. Thomas Hobbes
B. John Locke
C. Baron de Montesquieu
D. Jean-Jacques Rousseau
Correct Answer: C — Baron de Montesquieu elaborated the doctrine in his 1748 treatise "The Spirit of the Laws" to ensure that legislative, executive, and judicial powers remain divided.
Question 3 · Pre-Colonial Administration

In the pre-colonial Oyo Empire, the council of kingmakers led by the Bashorun who acted as a constitutional check on the Alaafin was called the:

A. Oyomesi
B. Ogboni
C. Are Ona Kakanfo
D. Ilari
Correct Answer: A — The Oyomesi was the supreme council of seven kingmakers in Old Oyo that checked the Alaafin's royal power and could mandate his abdication if he abused authority.
Question 4 · Constitutional History (Federalism)

Nigeria officially adopted a federal system of government with regional premiers and autonomous regional legislatures under the:

A. Clifford Constitution of 1922
B. Richards Constitution of 1946
C. Lyttelton Constitution of 1954
D. Independence Constitution of 1960
Correct Answer: C — The Lyttelton Constitution of 1954 formally established federalism in Nigeria, dividing powers into Exclusive and Concurrent lists and creating the office of Premier for each region.
Question 5 · International Relations

The permanent administrative headquarters of the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) Commission is located in:

A. Accra, Ghana
B. Dakar, Senegal
C. Abuja, Nigeria
D. Lome, Togo
Correct Answer: C — The ECOWAS Commission headquarters is situated in Abuja, the Federal Capital Territory of Nigeria.
