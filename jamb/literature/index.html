Subject Overview

JAMB Literature in English is a core prerequisite for Law, English and Literary Studies, Theatre and Performing Arts, Mass Communication, and Linguistics. The 40 questions examine literary appreciation, technical devices (metaphor, oxymoron, hyperbole, irony), dramatic concepts (soliloquy, dramatic irony, catharsis), and prescribed prose, drama, and poetry texts.

Key Topics for JAMB Literature in English

These core topic clusters feature regularly across UTME past questions and curriculum guidelines.

Figures of Speech & Literary Devices

Simile, metaphor, personification, hyperbole, oxymoron, paradox, litotes, irony, and synecdoche.

Elements of Drama & Stagecraft

Dramatic irony, soliloquy, aside, tragic flaw (hamartia), catharsis, proscenium stage, and conflict resolution.

Poetic Forms & Sound Devices

Sonnets, odes, elegies, ballads, alliteration, assonance, onomatopoeia, meter, and rhyme schemes.

Narrative Techniques in Prose

Point of view (first-person, third-person omniscient), stream of consciousness, flashbacks, and characterization.

African & Non-African Prescribed Texts

Thematic analysis, character development, cultural context, and stylistic analysis of official set works.

Sample JAMB Literature in English Questions & Explanations

Review these verified practice questions with step-by-step reasoning to understand how questions are framed in the UTME.

Question 1 · Figures of Speech (Metaphor vs Simile)

Identify the literary device used in the sentence: "The classroom was a noisy marketplace until the principal walked in."

A. Simile
B. Metaphor
C. Personification
D. Hyperbole
Correct Answer: B — A metaphor makes a direct comparison between two unrelated entities without using connecting words like "as" or "like".
Question 2 · Dramatic Conventions (Soliloquy)

When an actor stands alone on stage speaking their innermost thoughts aloud to the audience, the speech is called a:

A. Dialogue
B. Soliloquy
C. Prologue
D. Aside
Correct Answer: B — A soliloquy is a dramatic monologue delivered by a character alone on stage to reveal internal conflicts and motives.
Question 3 · Poetic Forms (Sonnet Structure)

A classic English or Shakespearean sonnet consists of how many lines and what stanza arrangement?

A. 12 lines in three quatrains
B. 14 lines in three quatrains and a rhyming couplet
C. 14 lines in an octave and a sestet
D. 16 lines in four stanzas
Correct Answer: B — The Shakespearean sonnet has exactly 14 lines divided into three quatrains (4 lines each) followed by a concluding rhyming couplet (abab cdcd efef gg).
Question 4 · Literary Terms (Tragic Concept)

In classical drama, the emotional release or purification of pity and fear experienced by the audience at the end of a tragedy is called:

A. Hamartia
B. Hubris
C. Catharsis
D. Nemesis
Correct Answer: C — Catharsis, defined by Aristotle in his Poetics, describes the emotional purging and moral renewal felt by the audience witnessing the hero's downfall.
Question 5 · Figures of Speech (Oxymoron)

Which of the following phrases represents an oxymoron?

A. Cruel kindness
B. As white as snow
C. The weeping willow bowed in prayer
D. Ten thousand saw I at a glance
Correct Answer: A — An oxymoron pairs two contradictory or contrasting terms side by side for paradoxical effect, as in "cruel kindness" or "deafening silence".
