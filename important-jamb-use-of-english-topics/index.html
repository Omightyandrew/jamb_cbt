15 useful revision areas

The numbering helps navigation only. It does not indicate frequency, importance ranking, or likelihood of examination.

  1. 1

    Comprehension: passage type and structure

    Recognise whether a passage narrates, describes, explains, or argues. Understanding its structure helps you follow how its ideas connect.

  2. 2

    Main ideas and supporting details

    Practise separating a passage's central point from examples, explanations, and minor details.

  3. 3

    Inference from textual evidence

    Draw a reasonable conclusion from what the passage says without adding unsupported assumptions.

  4. 4

    Tone and author's purpose

    Notice word choice and argument to decide how a writer feels and what a passage is trying to do.

  5. 5

    Summary and synthesis

    Identify key points and express them concisely while keeping the original meaning.

  6. 6

    Vocabulary in context

    Use surrounding words and the passage's meaning to interpret unfamiliar vocabulary and expressions.

  7. 7

    Synonyms and antonyms

    Choose words with a similar or opposite meaning that also fit the sentence or context.

  8. 8

    Idioms and figurative expressions

    Interpret an expression according to its intended meaning rather than reading every word literally.

  9. 9

    Sentence completion

    Use grammar and meaning together to find a word or phrase that makes a complete, coherent sentence.

  10. 10

    Concord

    Check how the subject relates to its verb, including when phrases or paired conjunctions make the sentence harder to parse.

  11. 11

    Parts of speech and grammar

    Recognise the role a word plays and how it relates to other parts of a sentence.

  12. 12

    Word formation and usage

    Study how word forms change and how a word's form affects its use in a sentence.

  13. 13

    Vowel sounds

    Practise distinguishing vowel sounds rather than relying on spelling alone.

  14. 14

    Consonant sounds

    Listen for consonant contrasts and practise identifying the sound represented in a word.

  15. 15

    Stress, rhyme, emphasis, and intonation

    Review how syllable stress, emphasis, rhyme, and changes in voice can affect spoken English.

Reading

Language use

Can I explain why a word fits the context and why a sentence is grammatically complete?

Oral forms
